MODULE 9 · Business

Marketing, Monetization and Entrepreneurship

A product nobody hears about earns nothing. Learn to find an audience, build in public and design a business that can sustain itself.

Build an audience while you build

Do not wait for launch day to find users. Share the work as it happens so an audience exists when the product is ready.

  • Pick one or two platforms where your users already spend time, such as LinkedIn, X or YouTube.
  • Post what you are learning and building: screenshots, short demos, lessons.
  • Be consistent. A small post every week beats a big one every few months.
  • Reply to people and join conversations, not only broadcast.

Find the problem first

  • Talk to ten potential users before writing code.
  • Look for a painful, frequent problem people already pay to solve or work around.
  • Narrow to a specific group. A product for everyone is marketed to no one.
💡 Tip: If people will not give you ten minutes to talk about the problem, they will rarely pay to fix it.

Business models

ModelHow it earnsWatch out for
SubscriptionMonthly or yearly planChurn; keep delivering value
Usage-basedPay per action or tokenBills that surprise users; show usage clearly
FreemiumFree tier, paid upgradeFree users still cost model spend
One-time purchaseSingle paymentOngoing costs with no ongoing revenue
Services or consultingCharge for time or outcomesDoes not scale without you

Check unit economics early: revenue per user minus the cost to serve them (hosting, model calls, support) must be positive.

Launch and learn

  • Launch a small version to a small group and collect feedback.
  • Track a few numbers: sign-ups, activation, retention and revenue.
  • Ship improvements weekly and tell users what changed.
  • Collect testimonials and case studies as proof for the next customers.

Entrepreneurial mindset

  • Treat ideas as hypotheses and test them cheaply.
  • Expect to be wrong often, and learn fast from it.
  • Focus on a customer outcome, not on technology for its own sake.
  • Combine skills: technical ability plus the ability to communicate and sell is rare and valuable.

Key takeaway: the best system design, the right tools and a repeatable process all serve the same goal, a product that solves a real problem and sustains itself.
