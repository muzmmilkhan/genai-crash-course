How LLMs Work

Look inside a large language model: tokens, embeddings, attention, and how it writes one word at a time.

The big picture

A large language model (LLM) is a very big pattern-completion machine. Given some text, it predicts what text is most likely to come next, then repeats.

Below is the architecture of a typical LLM. Click any box to learn what it does, or press the play button to follow your text through the model.

Step 1: Tokens

The model cannot read letters or words directly. Text is chopped into small pieces called tokens, and each token gets a number.

  • A token is often a word or part of a word.
  • About 4 characters, or three-quarters of an English word, per token.
  • Usage, limits and prices are measured in tokens.

Step 2: Embeddings, words as coordinates

Each token number is turned into a long list of numbers called an embedding. Words with similar meanings get similar numbers, so they sit near each other, like cities on a map.

This is why a search can find "automobile" when you type "car".

💡 Tip: The real space has hundreds or thousands of dimensions. The next slide squashes it to 3 so you can explore it.

Embeddings: 3D Word Space

Explore a map of word meanings. Related words cluster together: animals with animals, foods with foods. Directions in the space also carry meaning, so you can do arithmetic with words.

Step 3: Attention, what should I focus on?

To understand a word, the model looks at the other words around it and decides which ones matter. This is called attention. In "The animal didn't cross the street because it was too tired", the word "it" refers to the animal, and attention helps the model work that out.

Step 4: Choosing the next word

At the end, the model gives a probability to every possible next token. Then one is picked. A setting called temperature controls how adventurous the pick is.

Context window: the model's short-term memory

The model can only consider a limited amount of text at once, called the context window. It includes your prompt, the conversation so far, any documents you add, and the answer it is writing.

⚠️ Careful: If something is outside the window, the model cannot see it. In long chats, early details can be forgotten.

Limits to remember

  • It predicts likely text. It does not "know" things the way people do.
  • It can invent facts (hallucination).
  • It only knows what was in its training data, up to a cutoff date, unless you give it fresh information.
  • The same question can get slightly different answers.
