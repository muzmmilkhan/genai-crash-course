Build a Portfolio That Gets You Calls

Why a portfolio, not just a resume, earns you the interview, and how to build one that holds a recruiter's attention in the first 30 seconds.

Why a portfolio beats a resume alone

When a recruiter has never heard of you, your portfolio is the source of truth about who you are. It backs up every claim on your resume with something they can click, read or watch.

  • Prepare it before you start applying, not after the first rejection.
  • A well-crafted portfolio separates you from candidates with similar resumes.
  • It gives recruiters a reason to spend more than a glance on your application.
💡 Tip: Think of the resume as the trailer and the portfolio as the film. The trailer only has to earn the click.

The 30-second window

Recruiters typically spend around 30 seconds on a first pass over a resume. Your only goal in that window is to leave a lasting impression and make them want to spend more time on your application.

Try it: you get 30 seconds with a sample resume. Spot what would make a recruiter stop reading.

What recruiters check in that first pass: your tagline, your most recent role, measurable outcomes, and links that prove you are active.

Common resume mistakes

  • Listing too many projects, especially small or insignificant ones. Pick the few that matter.
  • Not being outcome-driven. Describe the result and impact, not just the task.
  • Going over two pages. One page is preferred, two is the maximum.
Weak bulletOutcome-driven bullet
Worked on a chatbot for supportBuilt a support chatbot that deflected 35% of tickets and cut first-response time from 4 hours to 5 minutes
Responsible for the data pipelineRebuilt the ingestion pipeline, reducing nightly processing from 6 hours to 45 minutes

⚠️ Careful: If you are not getting interview calls, the resume is most likely the problem, not your skills or experience. The resume's only job is to convince someone to call you.

Resume building principles

  • Start from a ready-made template (Microsoft Word or an online resume builder). Do not spend days on design.
  • Add a one-line tagline that sums up your expertise.
  • Tailor your skills to the role: technical, leadership or business.
  • Focus on recent, relevant experience, roughly the last 2 to 3 years.
  • Highlight achievements and outcomes for each role.
  • Include education, certifications and other credentials.
Example tagline:
GenAI engineer who ships production RAG and agent systems, from prototype to monitored deployment.

Use AI to refine your resume

ChatGPT, Claude and similar tools are good at tightening language, fixing formatting and tailoring a resume to a job description. You stay in charge of the facts: never let a model invent experience or numbers.

You are an expert technical recruiter. Here is my resume and a job description.

1. Rewrite each bullet to lead with the outcome and include a metric where one is already stated.
2. Suggest a one-line tagline for this role.
3. List skills in the job description that my resume does not show.
4. Do NOT invent any experience, tools or numbers. Ask me if something is missing.

💡 Tip: Run the same resume through several prompts and compare. Different job descriptions deserve different prompts.

Multiple versions and A/B testing

One resume rarely fits every role. Keep 4 to 5 versions, each aimed at a specific kind of job, for example technical lead, architect, manager or AI engineer.

Then treat it like an experiment: send each version to a similar number of roles and track which one produces interview calls.

⚠️ Careful: Change one thing at a time, and use enough applications per version before drawing conclusions. A handful of applications is noise, not evidence.

YouTube as a differentiator

Few candidates publish videos, so a short one stands out. It lets a recruiter hear how you explain ideas and what you are like to work with, which a resume cannot show.

  • Keep videos short: a 3 to 5 minute project demo is enough.
  • Cover what the project does, how you built it, and what the result was.
  • Link the videos from your resume, LinkedIn and portfolio site.
  • Do not wait for perfect production. Clear and honest beats polished.
