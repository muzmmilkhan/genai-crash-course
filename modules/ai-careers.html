AI Careers & Interview Prep

Prepare for GenAI interviews and build a portfolio that stands out: why the market wants AI generalists, what to learn, what to avoid, and how interviews have changed.

The shift: from LLM user to AI generalist

The job market is moving away from people who only use LLMs toward people who can build and run complete AI products. Roles are growing from AI product engineer to AI system architect, and ultimately to the AI generalist: someone who understands LLMs, product engineering, agentic systems and cloud infrastructure.

The AI generalist skill map

Ten areas come up again and again in hiring conversations. You do not need to be an expert in all of them, but you should be able to talk about each one with practical examples.

  • Productionizing AI systems: cloud infrastructure, cost, scalability and security.
  • Modern RAG pipelines for large knowledge bases and unstructured data.
  • Agentic workflows: access control, security and decision-making.
  • LLMOps: hosting, inference cost and token consumption.
  • Cloud deployment with AWS, Azure or GCP, plus infrastructure as code such as Terraform.
  • AI infrastructure: GPUs, TPUs, inference and training costs.
  • Evaluation and monitoring in production.
  • AI security and data privacy.
  • Cost optimization for AI tools and cloud resources.
  • Multimodal orchestration: coordinating different models for dedicated tasks.

What to avoid

  • Thinking GenAI is just prompt engineering.
  • Learning framework names (LangChain, AutoGen and others) without ever applying them.
  • Showing only simple chatbot projects, or copy-pasting existing AI apps.

How interviews have changed

Classic interviews focused on data structures and algorithms, SQL and ML theory. GenAI interviews increasingly test whether you understand the technology and can build with it, usually across three rounds.

Old styleNew style
DSA puzzlesAI fundamentals: transformers, tokens, embeddings, RAG
SQL drillsBuild discussions: architecture, data flow, scaling, cost, security, monitoring
ML theoryAI system design and live building

Practise the three rounds

Pick a round, read the question, answer out loud, then reveal the outline of what a strong answer covers.

Why cloud knowledge matters

AI systems live in the cloud, so you need to know which services do what. Infrastructure-as-code tools such as Terraform can automate setup, but they only help if you understand the components you are creating.

# Terraform: describe infrastructure as code (illustrative)
resource "aws_s3_bucket" "rag_documents" {
  bucket = "my-rag-documents"
}
💡 Tip: Know the concepts first (compute, storage, networking, secrets, monitoring). Then tools like Terraform are just a way to write them down repeatably.

TopicWhat to understand

InferenceRunning a trained model to answer requests. Dominant cost for most applications.
Training / fine-tuningMuch more expensive than inference. Often unnecessary if prompting and RAG are enough.
Managed vs self-hostedManaged APIs are simple; self-hosting adds control and operational work.

AI interviewers

Some companies now use AI systems to run parts of the interview. They are still evaluating the same things: practical skill and clear thinking.

  • Answer concretely, with examples from things you built.
  • Think out loud and explain trade-offs.
  • Be ready to defend your choices when asked follow-up questions.
  • Do not rely on memorized definitions. Show reasoning.
